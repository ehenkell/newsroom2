Literacy Crisis Rising due to ChatGPT

The Launch of ChatGPT

ChatGPT, one of the newest and most popular AI platforms, is changing the way students learn and retain information. At the beginning of 2022, OpenAI launched ChatGPT, an interactive AI program that assists people with everyday tasks and can provide answers to any question. If someone is not satisfied with the answer they were given, they can prompt or redirect it until they find exactly what they are looking for. Between January and May of 2025, 69% of all U.S. high school students used ChatGPT for homework and school-related assignments. As time goes on, this number seems to only be increasing.

Why Students Choose ChatGPT in Place of Real Learning

ChatGPT is easy for any student to use, regardless of age and grade. All they need access to is the internet and a device to search on. This makes it simple to open their computer and type a couple of words into the search engine. All of a sudden, they have easy access to the answers to their homework, and an entire essay can be written for them in approximately 30 seconds. When interviewed, students said they use ChatGPT due to their intense workload at school. In general, academic pressure is rising. Students are expected to have straight A’s, be involved in clubs and extracurriculars, and get into a prestigious college. Therefore, when they face excessive pressure to be the best at everything and simply can’t handle it, ChatGPT is just a quick search away.

Teacher's Initial Concerns

After its initial launch, schools quickly became worried about how students would use ChatGPT. Research conducted by Study.com reported that 43% of teachers were concerned their jobs were about to become much harder. They also became nervous about how easy it would become to cheat or plagiarize using ChatGPT, since students might not even realize they are doing so. Other teachers believe it can help them create fewer lesson plans and find new ways to improve classroom instruction. Instead of spending time creating assignments, they can spend more time with their students; however, they also recognize its many risks.

Every time someone opens ChatGPT, a small line of grey text appears at the bottom of the page: “ChatGPT is AI and can make mistakes.” Therefore, it does not always provide students with correct information; it can be biased, provide wordy answers, and lack human insight. The chatbot cannot tell if it's wrong, and it can’t check its own answers. Regardless, many students continue to use it for school even though it should not be trusted as an accurate academic source. ChatGPT isn't necessarily doing anything wrong when it responds to people’s searches. It’s the fact that students trust whatever it says without checking. A seventh-grade teacher reported that her students were reading way below their grade level. She explained: “I’m saying there is a literacy crisis. These kids cannot read. They cannot write.” The assessment levels required to pass have been lowered so dramatically that they’ve become nearly meaningless.

The Crisis in Action

Every time a student opens ChatGPT, their level of creative thinking slightly diminishes.

Already, literacy rates have been declining since 2013. Even before ChatGPT, increased technology use at school and at home has contributed to the literacy crisis, which already makes students more likely to fall behind in school. The current literacy rate is approximately 79%; the last time the rate was this low was during the 1870’s. Additionally, many schools don’t have technology regulations regarding AI or have only reinforced them recently. This has taken away students' ability to be creative and turn in an assignment they actually worked hard on. Not to mention, ¼ of eighth graders are below their appropriate reading level and are currently struggling to summarize a reading’s main ideas into a few sentences. When given an assignment that requires excessive reading or writing, students often go straight to ChatGPT. This takes away the time they need to absorb new information and engage in critical thinking, and the opportunity to create new ideas on their own is taken away. If students continue using ChatGPT the way they currently do, literacy rates are projected to only get worse.

Fox Business recently explained that ChatGPT allows students to put less effort into assignments and use it to complete homework and essays in a shorter amount of time. This puts students into a cycle; they don’t have to think through what they write anymore, don’t have to use brainpower, and ChatGPT can help students complete any assignment. After this easy and short process, more students are continuing to use it. Many students do not take the time to check what they submit anymore; teachers can often tell right away since they can see ChatGPT’s signature at the bottom of assignments. College students are using it to earn an entire degree; however, what many students don’t realize is that it slowly lowers their brain activity.

Possible Solutions

While some large school districts such as New York City and Los Angeles have gone so far as to ban ChatGPT in schools, many experts say this isn’t always the smartest option. It is available, free in most places, and is only becoming more popular. When conducting research, especially, students should be advised not to start with ChatGPT. An anonymous person asked ChatGPT: “In many cases, if I want quick, factual information, wouldn’t it just be better to check the Encyclopedia Britannica, especially given your tendency to produce plausible misinformation that, as you say, needs to be fact-checked by me?” It responded: “You’re absolutely right that for quick, factual information, especially when accuracy is paramount, consulting a reliable and authoritative source like the Encyclopedia Britannica is often a better choice. Encyclopedias are carefully curated and edited by experts, ensuring that the information is accurate, well-researched, and trustworthy.” In this conversation, it acknowledges that it is wrong, which is why students should go straight to peer-reviewed sources instead. To learn proper critical reading, students need to slow down, process, and ask questions about what they’re reading, which is the opposite of what ChatGPT does for students. It cannot teach a student these fundamental life skills.

The New York Times recommends that students and teachers use it to create outlines or make digital flashcards, but it should be prohibited for writing full essays. In many schools, it is up to the teacher or board of administration to teach students how to use ChatGPT appropriately. They must remind students that it should not be used for literacy skills such as summarizing articles. The question then becomes: How many students will actually listen?
